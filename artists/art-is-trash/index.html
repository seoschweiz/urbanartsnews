Art Is Trash is the street-art identity of Spanish artist Francisco de Pájaro, an artist closely associated with Barcelona's independent urban-art scene. Born in Zafra, Spain, he eventually settled in Barcelona, where the streets became an important part of his artistic practice.

Rather than limiting his work to traditional canvases and galleries, Art Is Trash became known for transforming materials abandoned in the city into spontaneous works of art. Cardboard boxes, discarded furniture, mattresses, rubbish bags and other unwanted objects can suddenly become characters, creatures and temporary urban sculptures.

The project Art Is Trash developed in Barcelona around 2009. Frustration with the conventional gallery system, economic insecurity and the financial crisis contributed to Francisco de Pájaro taking his work directly into public space. The street offered him something that traditional exhibition spaces could not: immediacy, freedom and direct contact with people.

Improvisation is central to his work. Instead of arriving with a finished composition, the artist responds to objects and situations that already exist in the urban environment. A discarded box, broken piece of furniture or pile of rubbish can provide the starting point for a new figure or scene.

This also makes much of his street work deliberately ephemeral. A work can appear in the evening and disappear when the city's rubbish is collected. Photography and social media therefore play an important role in documenting artworks that may physically exist for only a short period of time.

Behind the humorous, grotesque and sometimes chaotic characters is often social criticism. Waste, consumption, inequality, the art establishment and everyday urban life become part of the visual language of Art Is Trash. Materials that society has rejected are temporarily given a new identity and returned to public attention.

Barcelona remains particularly important to the story of Art Is Trash. Neighbourhoods including the Raval and the Gothic Quarter provided an urban environment where temporary interventions could become part of everyday street life rather than objects separated from the public inside a gallery.

The project has also travelled beyond Barcelona. Francisco de Pájaro has created street interventions in other cities, including London, bringing the same spontaneous approach to different urban environments.

Urban Arts News documents selected works, Instagram posts and stories connected with Art Is Trash, preserving a digital record of an artistic practice whose physical works are often designed to disappear.
