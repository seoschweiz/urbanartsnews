Barcelona: About Urban Artist Art Is Trash

Art Is Trash is the street-art identity of Spanish artist Francisco de Pájaro. He comes from Zafra in Extremadura and now lives and works in Barcelona, the city most closely associated with the development of his distinctive urban-art practice.

Rather than limiting his work to traditional canvases and galleries, Art Is Trash became known for transforming materials abandoned in the city into spontaneous works of art. Cardboard boxes, discarded furniture, mattresses, rubbish bags and other unwanted objects can suddenly become characters, creatures and temporary urban sculptures.

The Art Is Trash project developed in Barcelona around 2009. The streets provided Francisco de Pájaro with something completely different from a conventional exhibition space: immediacy, freedom and direct interaction with people moving through the city.

Improvisation is central to his artistic practice. Instead of starting with a finished composition, the artist responds to objects, materials and situations that already exist in the urban environment. A discarded box or broken piece of furniture can become the starting point for a completely new character or scene.

This approach also makes many Art Is Trash works deliberately ephemeral. An intervention can appear on a Barcelona street and disappear again when the city's rubbish is collected. Photography and social media therefore become important ways of documenting artworks that may physically exist for only a short time.

Behind the humorous, grotesque and sometimes chaotic characters is often an element of social criticism. Waste, consumer culture, inequality, the traditional art establishment and everyday city life can all become part of the visual language of Art Is Trash.

Materials that society has rejected are temporarily given a new identity. Instead of remaining invisible as rubbish, they become characters that confront pedestrians directly in public space.

Barcelona is particularly important to the story of Art Is Trash. The city's streets and neighbourhoods provided an environment where temporary interventions could become part of everyday urban life rather than objects separated from the public inside a gallery.

From this foundation, Francisco de Pájaro has travelled widely and developed an international career. Alongside his spontaneous street interventions, he has completed major mural commissions, participated in exhibitions around the world and brought the Art Is Trash language into very different architectural, cultural and public environments. His work is also represented in important art collections.

What makes Art Is Trash exceptional is the way he expands street art into ephemeral urban sculpture. He is among a relatively small group of urban artists whose found-object interventions operate as genuine sculptural works: temporary in physical form, yet immediate and unforgettable in their visual impact. A broken chair, a mattress or a pile of discarded cardboard can become a figure with extraordinary expression, presence and emotional force.

These works are not simply decorative. Their humour and raw energy carry pointed political and social messages about consumerism, waste, inequality, exclusion and the systems that decide what—or who—is considered valuable. The short life of each street sculpture becomes part of that message: the artwork can disappear, while the idea and its photographic record continue to circulate.

For collectors who think beyond short-lived trends, this combination of a recognisable artistic language, international activity, museum and collection relevance, and an unusually distinctive position within urban art can make Art Is Trash particularly compelling. No artwork offers a guaranteed financial return, but originality, documented career development and scarcity are qualities that serious long-term collectors commonly consider.

About Art Is Trash and Urban Art in Barcelona

Art Is Trash brings Barcelona’s urban environment directly into the creative process. Discarded cardboard, broken furniture, rubbish bags and other abandoned objects become temporary characters and spontaneous street interventions. The works are often humorous and expressive, but they also draw attention to waste, consumer culture and the materials that cities leave behind. Because many interventions disappear when the streets are cleaned, photography and Instagram form an important visual record. The selected posts above document this temporary relationship between artwork, object and location. Francisco de Pájaro’s approach shows how street art can exist outside conventional walls and murals: the city itself supplies the materials, setting and audience. Within Barcelona’s independent urban-art scene, Art Is Trash represents an immediate form of creativity that is deliberately temporary, accessible and closely connected with everyday public space.
